# v0.5 plan: graphics and animation overhaul

**Goal:** take Afterhours 16 from "procedural athletes that move believably" to **motion-captured movement on realistic, game-quality bodies**, while keeping the original look (night parks, team-branded Pro-Am, no licensed assets) and still running on a laptop GPU in a browser window.

v0.4 already moved the procedural motion onto measured numbers (see the table in `FIDELITY.md`). v0.5 replaces the motion itself with recorded human motion and keeps the procedural layer for what it is good at: exact gameplay timing, ball contact and foot planting.

## 1. Motion data: what we can use

No capture data was downloaded during v0.4. The build environment's network allowlist blocked these hosts, so the sources below come from research and still need to be fetched in a session that allows them, or downloaded by hand into `tools/mocap/raw/`.

| Source | What's in it | License (verify before shipping) | Plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CMU Graphics Lab Motion Capture Database** (mocap.cs.cmu.edu) | ~2,600 clips in ASF/AMC, with community BVH conversions. Subject 06 is basketball (dribbling, shooting, layups), plus plenty of walk, run, jump, sidestep and turn clips | Free to use, including inside commercial products. The data itself can't be resold | **Primary source.** Basketball set from subject 06; locomotion from the walk, run, jump and sidestep subjects |

## 2. Animation pipeline

**Offline (Python, `tools/mocap/`)**

1. **Import:** ASF/AMC and BVH parsers.
2. **Retarget** onto our 21-bone rig (`char/skeleton.js`):
   - a bone map plus rest-pose alignment, with the root and limb lengths scaled by the target's height and wingspan
   - an IK pass so feet and hands land where the source's did, relative to the floor and the ball
3. **Clean up:**
   - foot-contact detection (height plus velocity thresholds), then foot locking
   - root-motion extraction: forward speed, turn rate, vertical motion
   - resample to 30 Hz
4. **Annotate:** sync markers (foot down L/R), plus gameplay markers for jumpers (dip bottom, takeoff, set point, release, landing), dribbles (ball contact) and layups (gather and takeoff steps).
5. **Pack:** quantized quaternion tracks into one binary file per set (`client/assets/anim/*.ahanim`), about 10–20 MB for the whole library.

**Runtime (`client/js/char/`)**

1. **Clip player and blend tree:**
   - a locomotion blendspace by speed and direction, phase-synced on the foot markers
   - an additive upper-body layer for ball handling
   - a separate full-body layer for actions
2. **Gameplay time-warp:** the simulation stays authoritative. A jumper clip is warped so its takeoff and release markers land exactly on the sim's `takeoff` and `releaseAt`, so the green window and the animation can't drift apart. The same applies to layup gathers and dunk slams.
3. **IK post-pass:** the existing `Rig` handles foot planting on uneven timing, ball hands on the real simulated ball, and look-at.
4. **Motion matching (stretch goal):**
   - features: the future root trajectory at 0.33, 0.66 and 1.0 s, foot positions and velocities, and hip velocity
   - search: brute force or a KD-tree over about 15 minutes of data (about 27k frames × 27 floats), every 0.1 s with inertialized transitions
   - this is what removes the remaining "gamey" turns and starts
5. **Fallback:** the v0.4 procedural animator stays as the fallback for missing clips and as the reference implementation of the timings.

## 3. Player models

- **Base mesh:** a CC0 MakeHuman/MPFB body, retopologized to our skeleton.
  - LOD0 about 14k triangles (body and head), LOD1 about 6k, LOD2 about 2k, picked by screen size.
  - Budget: 10 on-court players plus 12 park NPCs within about 250k character triangles.
- **Shape:** morph targets for height, weight, muscle and wingspan, replacing today's procedural lofts. Face blendshapes for jaw, cheekbones, nose, brow and lips replace the four sculpted face shapes, so faces read as individual people.
- **Skin:**
  - 2K albedo, normal and roughness sets per skin-tone family, generated and baked by our own tools rather than scanned
  - pre-integrated subsurface scattering (a lookup texture) on top of the v0.4 two-lobe specular
  - a cavity/AO map for pores
- **Eyes, hair, brows:** a separate cornea and iris with refraction; eyebrow and eyelash cards; hair cards with alpha-to-coverage instead of v0.4's alpha-test shell.
- **Clothing:**
  - jerseys and shorts as separate meshes with baked wrinkle normal maps
  - a light verlet cloth on the jersey hem and shorts legs, LOD0 only
  - modeled shoe meshes per shoe line
- **Look:** keep the Afterhours style (night parks, saturated team colors, bloom) and aim for "late-console sim", not photoreal.

## 4. Physics realism follow-ups

- Contact: shoulder-to-chest bumps and screens as short physically-blended reactions, an additive "hit" on the spine and pelvis scaled by relative momentum.
- Use the SportVU-derived speed and acceleration distributions to re-tune `player.js` limits by position.
- Ball spin on the dribble: real backspin on hand contact, and loose-ball rolls that use the measured friction.

## 6. Risks

- **Licenses:** only CC0, CMU-style and our own data ships. Anything unclear stays out.
- **Retarget quality:** the CMU actors aren't elite basketball players, and the ball isn't tracked. The IK pass and our own ball contact cover that, and shooting form will still need hand-tuned keys.
- **Download size and load time:** keep the animation set to about 20 MB and stream LOD1/LOD2 first.
- **GPU budget:** the park renders three live games at once. LODs and the face-texture LOD from v0.4 are the levers.
