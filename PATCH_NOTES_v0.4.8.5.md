# Afterhours 16 — v0.4.8.5 Patch Notes

v0.4.8.5 rebuilds the three affiliation parks, adds Pro-Am ranks from Bronze to Grandmaster, keeps a new build at its home park until Pro 1, and lets you set a screen off the ball. It also starts the next Pro-Am and Pro Run game by itself, adds 1-minute Pro Run quarters, retunes seven badges, doubles the Sharp Eye hop and fixes Hall of Fame badges that didn't move up.

Everyone has to be on v0.4.8.5 to play online together. Pull the update before you connect.

## Pro-Am ranks: Bronze to Grandmaster

Every build has a Pro-Am rank, earned with **RP (ranked points)**:

| Rank | RP |
|---|---|
| Bronze | 0 |
| Silver | 400 |
| Gold | 800 |
| Platinum | 1,200 |
| Diamond | 1,600 |
| Master | 2,000 |
| Grandmaster | 2,400 and up |

### How RP is paid out after every game
The game service works RP out after every Pro-Am game you finish, from three things:
- **Win or loss:** +10 for a win, −6 for a loss.
- **Competition strength:** your RP against the other team's rating. Beating a stronger team pays more; losing to a weaker one costs more. Against an even team this part is ±15, and it goes up to about ±30.
  - Against an AI team, the rating follows how tough that team is. Online, it's the average of the five players you faced: real players count their own RP.
- **Personal performance:** your game score (points, shooting, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, fouls and turnovers) over a 20-minute game. A solid line is about even; a great one adds up to +12, and a bad one costs up to 8.

A win always pays at least 5 RP. A loss costs at least 2 and at most 40. RP never goes below 0.

**Leaving:** Forfeit & leave in a Pro-Am game costs 20 RP, as does leaving one running and starting another game. Losing your connection costs nothing.

**Builds from before ranks** start with RP from their Pro-Am record: 25 for every win and −15 for every loss, between Bronze and Gold (0 to 1,199). New builds start at Bronze.

### Where you see it
- **The Pro-Am screen** has a rank card: your rank badge, RP, the last game's change, a bar to the next rank, your best RP and this build's Pro-Am record.
- **The results** show the RP you won or lost and your rank, with the breakdown (result, competition, performance). A new rank gets a PROMOTED TO GOLD (or DEMOTED) banner.
- **Online,** each squad member's rank shows in the squad list and on the Pro-Am screen.

### Friends play within 2 ranks
Online, friends can run Pro-Am as one squad as long as every two of them are within **2 ranks** of each other. Silver plays with Bronze, Gold and Platinum; Bronze can't run with Platinum. If someone in the squad is too far apart, the game doesn't start and the message says who. The same check applies to Pro-Am challenges your squad sends or accepts. The AI teammates who fill open spots don't have ranks, and park games aren't affected.

- **A new build plays at its home park until it reaches Pro 1** (Rep level 5). On the Park screen the other two parks show **🔒 PRO 1** until then, and following your squad leader or a friend to another park waits until you get there.
- **From Pro 1 on,** every park is open to visit whenever you like.

## Gameplay

### Set a screen off the ball
- **Hold B** (Circle on PlayStation, **R** on the keyboard) when you're on offense without the ball. It's your bounce-pass button, so a remap moves it too.
- Your player goes to the ball handler's defender, plants beside him and holds the screen, chest on, for as long as you hold the button. Let go and you're free to roll or pop.
- It's a called, set screen: it hits harder than walking into someone. An AI ball handler uses it to come off your screen, as he does with his bigs' screens.
- With no teammate on the ball, or nobody guarding him, you plant where you are.
- Your player gets a screen stance (low, wide, arms crossed). The AI bigs do too when they set one.

### Brick Wall counts screens
**Brick Wall** now levels up from **successful screens**: screens that catch the defender, not just brushing past him. It used to level up from games played. Bronze takes 25, Silver 90, Gold 220 and Hall of Fame 450. Your progress carries over at the same share of the way to your next tier, and a tier you've already earned is never taken away. Successful screens show in your stats.

### Pro-Am and the Pro Run start the next game by themselves
- **After the results, the next game starts in 10 seconds,** just like the park. The button counts down (Next game · 9, 8…). Press it to go now, or pick the menu to stop.
- **In the Pro Run** that's the next game on your schedule: the next college game, your next regular-season game, or your next playoff game. When there's nothing to play next (draft night, waiting on the rest of the league, out of the playoffs, the offseason), it goes back to the Pro Run screen.
- **Online Pro-Am:** the squad leader's next game starts for the whole squad. A squad mate's screen goes back to the Pro-Am menu and joins when the leader starts.
- Placing new cap breakers still stops the countdown, as in the park.

### 1-minute quarters in the Pro Run
The Pro Run's quarter length (games you play) now starts at **1 minute**: 1, 3, 5, 8 or 12. Overtime in a 1-minute game is 1 minute too.

### Hall of Fame that didn't move up: fixed
- **Handles for Days** now counts every game your build has played. A build that had played 115 games but whose badge showed less is credited in full.
- **Badges move up as soon as you log in.** A badge whose progress already meets a requirement used to wait for the next game. Now it moves up the moment your profile loads, with the cap breakers or Icon badge that come with it. A message says what moved up, for example "Handles for Days is now Hall of Fame". That includes every badge lowered in this update.
- The usual rules still apply. A badge stops at the highest tier your archetype and height allow (Handles for Days, for example, tops out at Gold from 6'10" to 7'0" and at Silver above that). A build holds at most 7 Hall of Fame badges. When a badge is past its Hall of Fame mark but the build already holds 7, MyPlayer → Badges now says so ("Hall of Fame limit reached") instead of leaving the bar full with no explanation.

### Sharp Eye
- **The Sharp Eye base hops twice as high.** The ball still comes out right at the top of the hop. The extra height is for show: the defense is measured as if you'd risen as high as before, so the badge doesn't make you harder to contest than it did.
- **The Sharp Eye pop-up is now the Hash-Slinging pop-up.** It's the same size (the larger Icon badge banner with the full-size badge art), the same animation, and on screen for the same time (twice as long as a normal badge). Its reticle now shimmers in the same moving spectrum as Hash-Slinging's slashes, and its iris turns at the same speed.
- **It shows when Sharp Eye made the green.** It pops up on a green release that only Sharp Eye's 5% made: one that would have just missed the window without the badge. We confirmed it in a shootaround: a release 50.7 ms late, inside the 52.0 ms window but outside the 49.5 ms one without the badge, went green and the Sharp Eye pop-up showed. A green that's green either way doesn't show it (the badge didn't make it), and every green that goes in still gets the backflip.

### Its own soundtrack (a first)
- **The park has a soundtrack of its own:** 74 minutes of 80s R&B (the mix you sent: New Edition, Janet Jackson, Keith Sweat, Zapp & Roger and more), split into **28 tracks**.
- **They play at the event park and nowhere else.** That includes the park games there. The menus, the other parks, the Crew HQ, Pro-Am and the Pro Run never play them. The event park plays nothing from the main soundtrack either.
- **A new shuffle every time you arrive at the park.** The tracks you heard most recently go to the back of the order. When you leave, the main soundtrack picks up the song it was playing, from where it was.
- **How the split was made:** the mix is one continuous DJ mix with no gaps between songs, so there were no silences to split at. The cuts are where the music changes most clearly: the key, the sound and the tempo all shift at once. Each cut is at the quietest moment within 4 seconds of the change, with a short fade in and out. That makes tracks of 1½ to 4½ minutes. A few cuts may fall in the middle of a DJ blend rather than exactly between two songs. The Now Playing ticker shows them as "80s R&B Jamz · Cut 1 of 28" and so on (the mix doesn't name its songs). To put song names on them, edit `client/audio/beat/tracks.json`.
- The tracks are in their own folder, `client/audio/beat`. Without that folder the event park is simply quiet, as the rest of the game is without its music.
