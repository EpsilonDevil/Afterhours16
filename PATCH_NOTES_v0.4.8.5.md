# Afterhours 16 — v0.4.8.5 Patch Notes

v0.4.8.5 rebuilds the three affiliation parks, adds Pro-Am ranks from Bronze to Grandmaster, keeps a new build at its home park until Pro 1, and lets you set a screen off the ball. It also starts the next Pro-Am and Pro Run game by itself, adds 1-minute Pro Run quarters, retunes seven badges, doubles the Sharp Eye hop and fixes Hall of Fame badges that didn't move up.

Everyone has to be on v0.4.8.5 to play online together. Pull the update before you connect.

## Pro-Am ranks: Bronze to Grandmaster

Every build has a Pro-Am rank, earned with **RP (ranked points)**:

| Rank | RP |
|---|---|
| Bronze | 0 |
| Silver | 400 |
| Gold | 800 |
| Platinum | 1,200 |
| Diamond | 1,600 |
| Master | 2,000 |
| Grandmaster | 2,400 and up |

### How RP is paid out after every game
The game service works RP out after every Pro-Am game you finish, from three things:
- **Win or loss:** +10 for a win, −6 for a loss.
- **Competition strength:** your RP against the other team's rating. Beating a stronger team pays more; losing to a weaker one costs more. Against an even team this part is ±15, and it goes up to about ±30.
  - Against an AI team, the rating follows how tough that team is. Online, it's the average of the five players you faced: real players count their own RP.
- **Personal performance:** your game score (points, shooting, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, fouls and turnovers) over a 20-minute game. A solid line is about even; a great one adds up to +12, and a bad one costs up to 8.

A win always pays at least 5 RP. A loss costs at least 2 and at most 40. RP never goes below 0.

**Leaving:** Forfeit & leave in a Pro-Am game costs 20 RP, as does leaving one running and starting another game. Losing your connection costs nothing.

**Builds from before ranks** start with RP from their Pro-Am record: 25 for every win and −15 for every loss, between Bronze and Gold (0 to 1,199). New builds start at Bronze.

### Where you see it
- **The Pro-Am screen** has a rank card: your rank badge, RP, the last game's change, a bar to the next rank, your best RP and this build's Pro-Am record.
- **The results** show the RP you won or lost and your rank, with the breakdown (result, competition, performance). A new rank gets a PROMOTED TO GOLD (or DEMOTED) banner.
- **Online,** each squad member's rank shows in the squad list and on the Pro-Am screen.

### Friends play within 2 ranks
Online, friends can run Pro-Am as one squad as long as every two of them are within **2 ranks** of each other. Silver plays with Bronze, Gold and Platinum; Bronze can't run with Platinum. If someone in the squad is too far apart, the game doesn't start and the message says who. The same check applies to Pro-Am challenges your squad sends or accepts. The AI teammates who fill open spots don't have ranks, and park games aren't affected.

## Gameplay

### Set a screen off the ball
- **Hold B** (Circle on PlayStation, **R** on the keyboard) when you're on offense without the ball. It's your bounce-pass button, so a remap moves it too.
- Your player goes to the ball handler's defender, plants beside him and holds the screen, chest on, for as long as you hold the button. Let go and you're free to roll or pop.
- It's a called, set screen: it hits harder than walking into someone. An AI ball handler uses it to come off your screen, as he does with his bigs' screens.
- With no teammate on the ball, or nobody guarding him, you plant where you are.
- Your player gets a screen stance (low, wide, arms crossed). The AI bigs do too when they set one.

### Brick Wall counts screens
**Brick Wall** now levels up from **successful screens**: screens that catch the defender, not just brushing past him. It used to level up from games played. Bronze takes 25, Silver 90, Gold 220 and Hall of Fame 450. Your progress carries over at the same share of the way to your next tier, and a tier you've already earned is never taken away. Successful screens show in your stats.

### Pro-Am and the Pro Run start the next game by themselves
- **After the results, the next game starts in 10 seconds,** just like the park. The button counts down (Next game · 9, 8…). Press it to go now, or pick the menu to stop.
- **In the Pro Run** that's the next game on your schedule: the next college game, your next regular-season game, or your next playoff game. When there's nothing to play next (draft night, waiting on the rest of the league, out of the playoffs, the offseason), it goes back to the Pro Run screen.
- **Online Pro-Am:** the squad leader's next game starts for the whole squad. A squad mate's screen goes back to the Pro-Am menu and joins when the leader starts.
- Placing new cap breakers still stops the countdown, as in the park.

### 1-minute quarters in the Pro Run
The Pro Run's quarter length (games you play) now starts at **1 minute**: 1, 3, 5, 8 or 12. Overtime in a 1-minute game is 1 minute too.

### Hall of Fame that didn't move up: fixed
- **Handles for Days** now counts every game your build has played. A build that had played 115 games but whose badge showed less is credited in full.
- **Badges move up as soon as you log in.** A badge whose progress already meets a requirement used to wait for the next game. Now it moves up the moment your profile loads, with the cap breakers or Icon badge that come with it. A message says what moved up, for example "Handles for Days is now Hall of Fame". That includes every badge lowered in this update.
- The usual rules still apply. A badge stops at the highest tier your archetype and height allow (Handles for Days, for example, tops out at Gold from 6'10" to 7'0" and at Silver above that). A build holds at most 7 Hall of Fame badges. When a badge is past its Hall of Fame mark but the build already holds 7, MyPlayer → Badges now says so ("Hall of Fame limit reached") instead of leaving the bar full with no explanation.

### Sharp Eye
- **The Sharp Eye base hops twice as high.** The ball still comes out right at the top of the hop. The extra height is for show: the defense is measured as if you'd risen as high as before, so the badge doesn't make you harder to contest than it did.
- **The Sharp Eye pop-up is now the Hash-Slinging pop-up.** It's the same size (the larger Icon badge banner with the full-size badge art), the same animation, and on screen for the same time (twice as long as a normal badge). Its reticle now shimmers in the same moving spectrum as Hash-Slinging's slashes, and its iris turns at the same speed.
- **It shows when Sharp Eye made the green.** It pops up on a green release that only Sharp Eye's 5% made: one that would have just missed the window without the badge. We confirmed it in a shootaround: a release 50.7 ms late, inside the 52.0 ms window but outside the 49.5 ms one without the badge, went green and the Sharp Eye pop-up showed. A green that's green either way doesn't show it (the badge didn't make it), and every green that goes in still gets the backflip.

## Quick patch 3 (Oct 16): ICON status, Open Arms, the Mash's soundtrack

### ICON status: the endgame
A build becomes an **ICON** once it has done everything a build can do:
- **Every cap breaker unlocked.** Cap breakers open at 90 max OVR, and the first 5 Hall of Fame badges bring 5 each, so 25 on most builds (fewer on a build that can't reach 5 Hall of Fames).
- **Every badge maxed** as far as this build can take it: each badge at its cap, all 7 Hall of Fame slots filled (or as many as the build can reach), and the rest at Gold.
- **99 overall.**

What it brings:
- **ICON goes in front of the archetype everywhere** the archetype shows: MyPlayer, the player list, the home screen, Stats, the social phone, rosters and the team intros. It's written in the same moving spectrum colours as the Icon badges ("ICON Stretch Big").
- **The Icon badge replaces the ring under you in every mode:** the park, event parks, Pro-Am, the Pro Run, the Crew HQ and online games, for every ICON player (you, teammates, opponents). It's your build's own Icon badge, with a spectrum rim that turns and a glow that pulses, plus a thin ring in your team colour (gold for you, blue for teammates, red for opponents online). Being hot or cold still takes its place while it lasts.
- **A dark aura.** It's a black and deep-violet flame aura that wraps the player and licks upward, with a faint purple edge so it reads on bright courts and at night. It follows him as he moves, crouches and jumps. It grows with the build's win streak, which counts wins in every mode together. A loss, a forfeit or leaving a game ends the streak.

| Win streak | Aura |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Hidden |
| 3–5 | Level 1: low dark wisps |
| 6–8 | Level 2: fuller, with embers rising |
| 9–11 | Level 3: bigger, a wide dark haze behind him and black crackles |
| 12+ | Level 4 (ultimate): about 2 m of roaring flame over his head, constant crackles and a dark shockwave rolling across the floor |

- In a game the aura uses the streak you brought into it. In the park it updates as soon as a game ends, and other people see your new level too.
- **MyPlayer → Badges** has a new ICON status card. It shows what's left before you get there (cap breakers, badges, overall). Once you're an ICON, it shows your aura level and how many wins it takes to reach the next one.

### Open Arms, fixed
**Open Arms** (the Stretch Big Icon badge) now does exactly what it says: "+3% to every shooting and defense attribute (can pass 99). Unlocks exclusive ultra-flashy rebound snags."
- **The +3%** was already applied in every mode, to all eight shooting and defense attributes. One spot capped it: post defense held Interior D at 99. That's fixed, so it now passes 99 everywhere. The Attributes screen shows a small "+3%" next to each boosted rating (for every Icon badge's boosts).
- **The rebound snags almost never showed.** Before, the snag only played when the jump had started as a rebound jump while the ball was in the air: in a test, 30 of 68 boards. Floor grabs and boards out of a block jump got the plain catch. Even then the ball jumped to his chest in one frame, so the rip was never seen.
- **Now every board an Open Arms holder secures off a shot is a snag:** offensive or defensive, on the floor or in the air, user or AI, online too (every screen plays the same snag). The ball stays in his hands through the whole move.
- **The landing gesture lasts at most half a second,** and moving, dribbling, passing, shooting or jumping cuts it short.

### Rebound Package: Open Arms
A new animation category, **Rebounds**, for Stretch Bigs who have unlocked Open Arms, and for nobody else. It's never in the store, it doesn't show in anyone else's Inventory, and the game refuses to equip it on anyone else ("Requires the Open Arms Icon badge"). It comes with the badge and is put on for you, including builds that already held Open Arms. It shows in the Inventory's Rebounds tab and as "Rebound package" under MyPlayer → Animations, where you can preview it.

Six all-new streetball snags, a different one on each board:
- **Chin Check:** ripped down to the chin, elbows swung hard both ways, then he throws his head back and screams.
- **Cuff & Holster:** cuffed one-handed at the top and swung straight-armed down to the hip, the other arm flung out; he lands pointing at the floor twice.
- **Pterodactyl:** legs split wide, the ball swung out to arm's length as both arms spread like wings; he lands holding the wings and gives them a flap.
- **360 Rip-Through:** a full turn in the air with the ball low through the hips, knees tucked; he lands in a crouch swinging it knee to knee.
- **Tomahawk Yank:** yanked behind the head with the opposite knee up, then whipped down; he lands bent over it, barking.
- **Hammer Down:** lifted overhead, hammered down on the landing and pounded twice, then a bicep flex with the ball on the hip.

With the package off, an Open Arms holder still gets a basic snag, the Open Arms Rip.

### Sharp Eye: the release matches the hop
The Sharp Eye base hops twice as high since v0.4.8.5. Now **the ball leaves at the top of that doubled hop, twice as high above his standing release point as on the old hop.** Before:
- **Quick shots let go early.** Pull-ups and moving shots let go up to 67 ms before the top. They now go at the top.
- **The store and MyPlayer preview** used its own timing and released up to 105 ms early. It now matches the game exactly. A Sharp Eye holder trying on another base in the store now sees that base, not his Sharp Eye jumper.
- **The heels floated** on the old hop's timing. They now peak at the top of the doubled hop.
- **AI Sharp Eye holders** now judge contests the way the defense measures them.

The green window, the release timing and the rule that the defense measures you as if from the old hop height are unchanged. Taking off a little sooner shortens the pump-fake window on quick Sharp Eye shots.

### Gameplay
- **Y / Triangle jumps for a rebound off the ball on offense.** It's the block / rebound button, so it now jumps for the board on offense too. It's only ever a jump: with a pass on its way to you, it won't turn into a catch-and-shoot the way the shoot button does. With the ball, Y is still the lob pass; on defense, still the block.
- **Half as many fouls in the park.** Shooting fouls and reach-ins are called half as often as before (0.375 of the Pro-Am rate, was 0.75). Pro-Am and the Pro Run are unchanged.

## Quick patch 4 (Oct 16): event parks keep their crowd through a handover

**The bug:** online players found And The Beat Goes On empty right after it took over from The Jungle Ballers at 5 pm Pacific, when the park should have been busy. The game decided which event was on **once, when it was started.** Anyone who had the game open through the switch kept sending the event's AI players to the park that had just closed. The Park screen did move on to the Beat, but its AI world still sent everyone to the Jungle, so the Beat stood empty. That lasted until the game was restarted. A game started after 5 pm was fine.
- Measured on the evening of the switch: with the event set right, about 30–35 AI players were at the Beat from 5 pm on (busy, as the schedule rules intend). With the stale setting there were none.

**The fix:**
- **The game now knows the whole event schedule,** not just the event that was on when it started. The AI world follows it to the second: the moment one event hands over to the next, the entrants go to the new park and the closed one empties.
- **The Park screen, its event card and the leaderboard switch on their own** within a few seconds of a handover, and the game tells you ("And The Beat Goes On is live now"). If you're standing in the park that just closed, it says so ("The Jungle Ballers has closed: its leaderboard is final").
- **The handover uses the server's clock,** so a PC clock that's a few minutes off still switches at the right moment. The server has the last word if the schedule is ever edited while people are playing.
- Tested live: a game started before a handover switched 8 seconds after it. The new event park then had 35 AI players online, all 6 courts in play and people walking around, and the old park had none.
